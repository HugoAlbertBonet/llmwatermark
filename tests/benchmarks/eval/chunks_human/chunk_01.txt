ANSWER A:
Enriched air, also known as compressed air diving or air diving, involves breathing a mixture of oxygen and nitrogen rather than the normal 100% oxygen used in traditional scuba diving. In this scenario, only the oxygen content is increased above atmospheric levels.

Divers choose to use enriched air for several reasons:

1. Reduced risk: Enriched air reduces the risk of decompression sickness (the "bends") because the higher concentration of nitrogen means that bubbles form more slowly during ascent.

2. Longer duration: With less nitrogen dissolved in their bloodstream, divers can stay underwater longer without risking high-pressure illness.

3. Simplicity: The equipment required for enriched air diving is simpler compared to full-depth diving due to the lower demand for inert gases like helium.
